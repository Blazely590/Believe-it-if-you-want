Zambia's economy this week: cheaper money, a tougher budget, a softer kwacha

19 November 2026 · Economy · 4 min read

Zambia is easing interest rates while preparing to raise taxes, and its currency has started to give back some ground. Here is where the main numbers stand, with sources at the end.

Inflation ticked up, but not because of food

Annual inflation rose to 6.3% in October from 6.1% in September, according to the Zambia Statistics Agency release of 29 October. Monthly inflation was 0.7%, up from 0.4%.

The rise came from non-food prices, mainly fuel, hotel services and bus and taxi fares. Non-food inflation climbed to 7.4% from 6.6%, while food inflation eased to 5.7% from 5.8%. The October figure is still inside the Bank of Zambia's 6% to 8% target band. The November figure, due late this month, will show whether October was a one-off.

The Bank of Zambia has been cutting

On 30 September the Bank of Zambia cut its policy rate by 250 basis points, from 13.25% to 10.75%. It was the fourth cut in a row. September inflation of 6.1% was the lowest since February 2018. The next rate decision is listed for 27 November, and the October uptick is one of the things the committee will weigh.

A new IMF programme, with strings

On 9 October IMF staff and the Zambian government agreed a new 36-month Extended Credit Facility worth about US$1.47 billion (SDR 1,076 million). It still needs approval from the IMF Executive Board. The IMF puts 2026 growth at 5.6%, and reports gross reserves of US$6.1 billion in September.

The agreement comes with conditions. The IMF wants the primary surplus to reach 3% of GDP by 2029, starting in 2027, temporary fuel-tax relief reversed, and non-priority capital spending scaled back. It also cites revenue shortfalls and higher-than-budgeted spending by the Food Reserve Agency as pressures on the 2026 budget.

The 2027 budget raises taxes

Finance Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane presented the 2027 budget on 6 November. The government aims to keep growth around 5% and cut the deficit from 5.9% of GDP in 2026 to no more than 3.0%. Copper output is projected at 930,000 tonnes in 2026, up from about 890,000 in 2025, with a goal of 3 million tonnes by 2031.

The tax proposals, which take effect on 1 January 2027 if Parliament approves them, are expected to raise about K10.8 billion:

  • VAT rises to 16.5% from 16%.
  • A new top PAYE rate of 39.5% applies to high earners. The speech gives the monthly threshold as K37,000, but the annex says K34,000, so check the final text before relying on either.
  • A 15% withholding tax on dividends paid by mining companies, up from 0%, and a 5% export duty on zinc concentrates.
  • Excise of K1 per kilogram on sugar and 20% on chocolate and sugar confectionery.
  • Higher mobile money levies and passport, residence and work permit fees.

Reports differ on how the corporate income tax increase is worded, so that measure is left out until the final text is published.

The kwacha lost ground

The kwacha fell 0.67% in the week to 13 November, its biggest weekly loss in nearly a month, and closed just below K20.50 to the US dollar. Demand for dollars kept running ahead of supply, and the dollar stayed near its strongest level since May 2025 on expectations that US interest rates will stay high.

What to watch next

  • November inflation, due late in the month: a second rise would be a warning sign.
  • The Bank of Zambia's rate decision, listed for 27 November.
  • Parliament's vote on the 2027 tax measures.
  • The IMF Executive Board decision on the new programme.
